IDR · FX and NDF trade views

Briefs · 2026-09-11 · One-time brief

Two explicit relative-FX recommendations were found: JPM long INR versus a 50/50 PHP–IDR basket, and Nomura long SGD/IDR. Both express IDR underperformance against other Asian currencies. Neither is a standalone USDIDR trade or a pure NDF-points recommendation. No explicit IDR points trade was verified in the selected sources.

The strongest common thread is that a tactical Indonesian bond rally can coexist with a cautious view of the currency. The research does not support treating a bond-duration long as an unhedged IDR long.

  • Original entry: 8 June 2026, basket index 100.00.
  • Target / stop: 104.00 / 98.00 in basket-index units. These are not exchange rates or NDF points.
  • Instrument, tenor, expiry and holding period: not specified for this row in the sections read. Do not borrow the maturity of an adjacent trade in the table.
  • Status: explicitly maintained as of 4 September; not independently reconfirmed on 11 September. Current basket mark and remaining target distance unavailable.
  • Rationale: JPM preferred INR's reserve/flow backdrop and saw less scope for sustained IDR outperformance given Indonesia's thinner external buffers, policy uncertainty, possible easing and corporate dividend-related dollar demand.

The report discusses a 10bp reduction in the seven-day repo auction rate to 6.4%, alongside falling SRBI yields. That is not evidence of a 10bp cut in the BI policy rate. JPM's broader USD-positive bias is context, not a separate explicit USDIDR trade instruction.

Assessment: This is the most fully specified IDR-related FX recommendation found because its table supplies entry, target and stop. It is still an old entry and a three-currency basket. A long INR/short IDR pair alone would change the published trade's risk and should not be represented as the same recommendation.

Asia Economic Monthly, 11 September 2026, publication 1319880, page 43, FX strategy.

  • Direction: buy SGD against IDR; benefit from SGD outperforming IDR.
  • Horizon: medium term.
  • Entry / target / stop / tenor / precise implementation: not stated in the excerpt.
  • Status: an explicit recommendation in an excerpt originally published 19 August 2026, republished in the September monthly. The monthly's date does not establish a new initiation or independent live-status check.
  • Rationale: policy uncertainty, BI independence/credibility concerns, sovereign-rating and equity-index risks, weaker FDI, a wider external deficit and limited reserve adequacy.

Assessment: This is a simpler two-currency expression of Indonesia-specific concerns, but the available excerpt lacks a usable trade ticket. It should remain a sourced idea for assessment rather than be presented with invented execution levels.

USDIDR forecasts and the policy disagreement

Nomura's current economics section on page 15, with data through 10 September, forecasts USDIDR at 17,550 at end-2026 and 17,350 at end-2027. The table specifies end-period exchange rates. These are macro forecasts, not entry or target levels for its SGD/IDR trade, and no contemporaneous spot quote was retrieved to calculate expected returns.

That section expects BI to hold its policy rate this month and then increase it by 50bp to 6.25% in Q4, citing balance-of-payments pressure and FX stability. It raises the 2026 current-account-deficit forecast to 2.1% of GDP. The older page-43 excerpt still refers to 2.0%; use the newer economics forecast for the current baseline.

This differs from JPM's emphasis on easier operating rates and the possibility of an easing cycle if global conditions permit. The sources are discussing different instruments and horizons, so the disagreement cannot be reduced to “one bank says BI has cut, another says it has hiked.” The actionable question is whether easier liquidity can persist without renewed currency pressure forcing policy restraint.

The Nomura forecast for lower USDIDR over a longer horizon also need not invalidate a shorter-horizon SGD/IDR relative trade. Different currencies, dates and horizons matter; the excerpt does not provide a joint forecast path sufficient to reconcile their expected returns quantitatively.

What was — and was not — found on NDF points

No explicit IDR NDF-points or forward-basis trade was verified. The material reviewed did not supply a buy/sell-points instruction with tenor, entry, target, stop and carry/roll assumptions. No claim is made that the banks have no such recommendation elsewhere.

For any later points trade, the minimum needed is its exact quote convention, tenor/fixing dates, onshore versus offshore instrument, whether spot exposure is hedged, and whether the stated return comes from points, basis convergence or currency direction. Under the usual USDIDR quote in rupiah per dollar, an outright forward combines the spot level and forward points; holding an outright NDF does not by itself isolate points exposure. Cross-currency recommendations may use NDF legs operationally without being points trades.

No points curve, live spread, executable quote or hedge-adjusted expected return was requested from Bloomberg. Source levels are retained as historical evidence, not refreshed pricing.

Catalysts and ways the thesis could fail

The following is my synthesis of the cited research, not an additional bank trade recommendation:

  • BI decisions versus liquidity operations: distinguish the policy rate, repo auctions, SRBI yields and FX intervention. Easier money-market conditions may help duration while reducing currency support; persistent external pressure could instead force tighter policy.
  • External funding: watch reserve adequacy, the current account, FDI and corporate dollar demand. Better funding and stronger credibility would weaken the structural short-IDR thesis.
  • Oil and global rates: renewed oil strength or tighter US financial conditions challenge Indonesian assets. Lower oil and a weaker dollar can support IDR and squeeze shorts; BNP's stop-out is a concrete reminder of this sensitivity.
  • The other side of the trade: JPM needs INR to outperform the combined PHP/IDR basket, not merely IDR to weaken. Nomura's expression also depends on SGD's behaviour and Singapore policy; an IDR-only macro view is insufficient.
  • Positioning and timing: crowded underweights can rally on limited good news. Old initiation levels cannot establish attractive risk/reward today.

My preferred next analytical step, if revisiting the topic later, would be to compare a fresh marked INR basket and SGD/IDR expression against the current IDR forward curve. This brief does not schedule further IDR research or initiate a trade.
