Tech weakness, firm oil · rates choices

13 September 2026 | Market data through 11 September close | Horizon: 14–18 September | Bloomberg requests: zero

My recommendation

German 10-year duration is my preferred receiving leg, but I would express it relative to UK 10-year duration rather than as an outright tech hedge. Long Bund / short Gilt, matched in USD-equivalent DV01, best combines the recent equity/oil relationships, the available fair-value evidence and the CTA model's covering asymmetry. This is a modest-conviction tactical preference; the evidence does not establish a reliably profitable weekly strategy.

Second choice: a US 5s30s flattener, long 30-year / short 5-year duration, DV01 matched. This avoids cross-currency exposure and has a cleaner curve fair-value anchor. It still carries Fed and term-premium risk.

If the constraint is an outright receiver, Bund 10Y goes first on the watchlist. I would wait for yields to start falling while oil stays firm. Tech weakness alone does not supply that confirmation. I would not choose KRW receivers solely because Korea is tech-sensitive, or INR receivers while oil is rising.

These are independent discretionary research conclusions. They are not signals from the frozen pullback paper tracker and have not been entered in it.

Why the direction of stocks is insufficient

Tech can fall because growth expectations weaken, because discount rates rise, or because valuations/positioning unwind within equities. Only the first naturally argues for broadly lower policy-rate expectations. Firm or rising oil can preserve inflation pressure through the same equity sell-off.

First choice: long Bund / short Gilt 10Y

Correlation and oil exposure. The six-month daily two-factor model puts Gilt oil sensitivity at approximately 0.91bp per +1% Brent, versus 0.55bp for Bunds. Both have recently sold off with tech weakness, but Gilts have been the more vulnerable leg. Regressing the matched spread directly gives about +2.8bp widening in Gilt minus Bund for Nasdaq −3% / Brent +5%, which benefits long Bund / short Gilt.

Fair value. At a Bund reference yield of 3.504%, Sadash's combined models show approximately 3bp cheap over six months, 10bp cheap over one year, and 10bp cheap over two years. The one-year residual z-score is +1.77, with an estimated 9.2-observation half-life. However, the five-year combined model has only a +2bp raw gap and a −1.04 rolling z-score, labelled rich. The conclusion is window-sensitive. I did not verify a like-for-like Gilt fair-value model or a dedicated Gilt–Bund spread fair-value model; this is valuation support for the Bund leg, not proof that both legs are mispriced.

CTA positioning. Sadash models Bund duration momentum at −0.94, exposure −1.88 SD versus its own trailing distribution. Gilt momentum is −0.757, exposure −1.21 SD. Both are short duration. In the saved five-day scenario grid, Bund buying begins around 3.451%, about a 5.3bp rally, while Gilt needs about 11.3bp, to 5.230%, merely to reach zero net model flow. That creates potential relative covering support if bonds start rallying; it does not mean covering has already been triggered. With yields unchanged, both models still sell duration.

Trade construction. Reference spread: Gilt 5.343% minus Bund 3.504% = 183.9bp. The trade benefits from widening. Match the two legs' USD-equivalent DV01, not face value; handle currency exposure and financing separately. Cash or appropriately risk-matched futures are the closest implementation to the data tested. EUR/GBP swaps introduce swap-spread/basis exposure; these cash-yield reference levels are not swap entry quotes.

Timing and invalidation. Prefer a small initial expression only if Bunds are already outperforming Gilts as tech weakens and oil stays firm; reassess after the BoE. A fall in UK yields led by a dovish UK policy repricing, a reversal lower in oil, or widening European-specific term/fiscal risk undermines the relative thesis. Current weekly residual volatility is about 5–7bp, larger than the central scenario gain, so this is not a high-confidence full-size position. No optimized numerical stop or target is claimed.

Second choice: US 5s30s flattener

At Friday's cached closes, UST 5Y is 4.7821% and 30Y 5.3522%, so 30Y minus 5Y is 57.01bp. Buy 30Y / sell 5Y in matched DV01; a narrowing spread makes money to first order.

  • Scenario: the six-month daily fit implies 2.7bp flattening for Nasdaq −3% / Brent +5%; with oil flat it implies 2.4bp flattening. Three-, six- and twelve-month daily fits agree on flattening; the two-year daily fit does not.
  • Fair value: Sadash's five-year curve model gives 38.3bp, versus 57.0bp observed: 18.7bp too steep, residual z +1.46. Its estimated half-life is 28 observations, so a complete move to fair value is not a next-week target.
  • CTA: model exposure is −1.79 SD in 30Y versus −2.07 SD in 5Y. The five-day zero-flow rally is only 4.8bp in 30Y, versus 18.4bp in 5Y. That supports the possibility of earlier long-end covering while the belly remains under selling pressure.
  • Failure mode: a sharp dovish Fed repricing can rally 5Y more than 30Y; a fiscal or long-end term-premium shock can sell 30Y disproportionately. Either steepens the curve against the trade. Relative DV01 matching does not eliminate convexity or curve-basis risk.

The more direct weekly six-month fit implies 2.6bp flattening but has about 6.0bp residual weekly volatility. Across the 14 historical tech-down/oil-firm weeks, flattening happened in 7 of 14; the median was just 0.8bp. This is supportive scenario construction and valuation, not demonstrated high win-rate alpha.

Why the other obvious receivers rank lower

KRW: the EUR–KRW 5y5y fair-value model does support receiving KRW against paying EUR: current EUR minus KRW is −80.9bp versus fitted −28.8bp, residual −52.1bp / z −2.91. But that is a forward-spread valuation model, not outright KRW 5Y fair value. On the actual weekly horizon, its recent scenario behavior goes against that trade. KRW 5Y model momentum is −0.622, the five-day flat-yield scenario still sells, and a rally of about 18.2bp is needed for zero model flow. The Asian IRS panel is not evidence of substantial real CTA holdings.

INR: the 5Y rate is 6.5825%, versus fair value 6.6701% over six months and 6.8340% over one year: approximately 9–25bp rich in price terms. Oil sensitivity also leans against receiving. Paying INR can be an oil expression, but receiving KRW against it has weak weekly support and substantial residual risk.

JGB: lower measured oil sensitivity does not automatically make it a tech hedge. The six-month two-factor fit explains only about 3% of daily yield variation. JGB is modestly rich in several combined fair-value windows; its CTA exposure is around its own historical mean despite strongly negative momentum. BoJ event risk is immediate.

Outright UST10: six- and twelve-month fair values are roughly on top of spot. Its modelled short positioning can fuel a rally, but requires a catalyst; it does not override the recent adverse equity/oil correlation.

Events, model limits and provenance

Next week includes the Fed on 15–16 September with projections, BoE on 17 September, and BoJ on 17–18 September. Those decisions can dominate a one-week correlation trade. Dates were checked against the Federal Reserve calendar, BoE policy page, and BoJ meeting schedule. No market prices were fetched from the internet.

All market data came from the local Bloomberg store. All ranked markets and scenario factors extend through 11 September. The additional INR 5y5y history ends on 9 September and is not used in the recommendations; its older cutoff remains recorded in the calculations. Sadash fair-value and CTA summaries were generated on 13 September around 19:13–19:15 Singapore. Source fingerprints and paths are in cache-audit.json; full calculations are in analysis.json. No original bank documents were downloaded or saved.

The analysis screens 21 outright markets, six forward rates and five relative expressions. It uses common observed weekdays without forward filling, checks 63/126/252/504-observation daily windows and 26/52/104-week windows, and uses HAC covariance estimates. Different markets close at different times; the one-day lag checks in the calculation file are a sensitivity check, not an identification of causality. Holiday gaps can make a common-date change cover multiple sessions. Weekly sampling reduces but does not eliminate this issue. Generic futures oil returns can contain roll effects.

CTA is a yield-based replication, not an independently verified positioning census. Its aggregate dollar calibration is an assumption, and Asian IRS dollar figures are explicitly non-investable proxies. The source also uses five staggered lookbacks for the current trend anchor but a single 252-day anchor in forward simulations. Consequently, the saved zero-flow thresholds are indicative model outputs, not verified execution triggers. Zero flow means no net change in model holdings over that path; it does not mean the model is flat or long duration. No CTA source code was changed.

Fair-value regressions are fitted to contemporaneous levels and often have highly persistent factors. High R² and fitted half-lives do not establish out-of-sample trading profits. Raw fair-value gaps and rolling residual z-scores can disagree. No cost, financing, futures CTD, swap basis or live quote check was performed. The recommendations therefore identify a preferred structure and what would invalidate it, rather than presenting an executable ticket or a guaranteed weekly payoff.
