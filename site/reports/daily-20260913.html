GS — CHF/EUR forward rates; Canadian curve exit

Global Rates Trader: Grouped Hikes, 11 September 23:28 BST / 12 September 06:28 Singapore. Read: regional narrative sections; image-only trade table not verified.

Explicit recommendation, first observed today: Pay CHF 1y1y against EUR 1y1y. Source prints entry 3.02, stop 3.25, target 2.65. Paying CHF and receiving EUR implies a narrowing EUR-minus-CHF forward-rate spread. Interpreting those numbers as percentage points would imply 302bp entry, 325bp stop and 265bp target, but the table's unit convention was not independently verified. Keep the printed numbers as source references, not an executable ticket. Initiation date, sizing, DV01 hedge ratio and horizon are not established.

GS argues Swiss rates have lagged the repricing of inflation risks and could catch up, particularly if the ECB tightens further.

Closed: CAD 2s10s steepener at its revised stop during the week. The narrative gives no exact exit level or P&L. GS now sees greater scope for Canadian 5y outperformance in 2s5s10s; that is a directional preference without a complete new ticket in the sections read.

GS raises year-end UST10 to 4.75% from 4.40%, UST2 to 4.30% from 3.80%, and Bund10 to 3.25% from 3.00%. These are forecasts, not trade-entry or stop levels. Near-term flattening risk remains if energy stays elevated, while its US medium-term bias is toward steepening as the policy path ultimately undershoots pricing.

Where the banks differ

USD into the Fed: JPM sees valuation support and retains modest USD longs versus SEK/CAD. GS Global FX Trader: The Walk After the Talk, 11 September 23:17 BST, sees downside-skewed policy risks because the hurdle for a hawkish surprise is high. Both acknowledge that the Fed's reaction function matters more than a hot inflation print by itself.

JPY: Both favour selective tactical JPY length. GS prefers EUR as a funder; JPM retains SEK/JPY shorts with a tighter profit stop. GS sees better long-term asymmetry for JPY versus USD than short-term; neither note provides a new fully specified USDJPY entry.

CAD/AUD: JPM favours AUD and remains bearish CAD, stressing carry and Canadian trade-policy damage. GS's combined rates-based systematic signals favour CAD/NZD and oppose AUD/GBP/USD. These are different frameworks and horizons, not two directly comparable trade tickets.

Fed path: Nomura September FOMC Preview, production complete 11 September 18:45 UTC / 12 September 02:45 Singapore, forecasts September and December 25bp hikes, then a 2027 hold. It expects median dots of 4.125% for both 2026 and 2027. GS retains a September-only baseline. Nomura's approximate timestamp recorded yesterday was 15 minutes later; this body timestamp is authoritative. Its 0.278% m/m August core PCE estimate and roughly 90% market-implied September hike probability are dated forecasts/market observations, not released data or today's prices.

French sovereign spreads: BNP retains a Q4 OAT-Bund forecast of 93bp and Q1 2027 100bp; GS raises its year-end OAT-Bund forecast to 85bp from 70bp. Both anticipate elevated spread risk; BNP's fly closure does not mean it expects outright French spreads to tighten structurally.
