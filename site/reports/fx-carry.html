Independent FX carry framework

Frameworks · 2026-09-13 · Independent revision

13 September 2026 | market close: 11 September | $500,000 loss from inception | 1–3 months

This revision supersedes the earlier discretionary recommendations. No bank's currency picks, weights or macro conclusions enter the selector. The previous book is a benchmark only. The independent model now ranks the full eligible universe, models six-month factor exposures, constrains net currency risk and separates ordinary volatility from gaps and realized losses. Cumulative new Bloomberg use: 48,528 cells; cached history supplies most factor data.

My preferred starting point is the DM book below. The optional mixed book exchanges $2m of GBP exposure for $0.75m MXN and $1.25m BRL. The choice is an explicit carry-versus-EM-risk trade-off. Neither portfolio is a claim of factor neutrality or a guaranteed $500k maximum loss.

1. The macro framework: when should carry be on?

Carry needs the interest differential to survive without a larger adverse spot move. The best environment combines resilient growth, contained inflation uncertainty, credible policy and orderly funding. The direction of rates alone is insufficient. A growth-driven increase in yields can accompany profitable carry; an inflation or funding shock can make the same increase hostile. Rate cuts can help in a benign disinflation but hurt in a recession-led flight to safety.

The economic mechanism is independently supported by research on funding conditions, risk appetite and leveraged carry unwinds. That does not establish precise trading thresholds. BIS: August 2024 market turbulence and carry unwind, NBER: Carry Trades and Currency Crashes.

The measurable gate implemented here

The lightweight historical gate uses US-centric proxies. It is not a complete global macro classifier:

  • Weak growth surprises: CESIUSD below zero and falling over 21 sessions. This measures surprises relative to expectations, not the level of GDP growth.
  • Inflation/policy repricing: 10-year US breakevens up more than 20bp and US two-year yields up more than 25bp over 21 sessions.
  • Liquidity stress: VIX above 30, or median DM FX realized vol over 20 sessions above 1.5 times its 126-session vol.
  • Normal multiplier 1.0; weak-growth or inflation condition 0.5; both conditions or liquidity stress 0.25; VIX above 40 zero. Observe at close, execute at next close. Allocation selection is monthly; this sizing gate can change daily.

At the current cut, CESIUSD is 27, up 4 over 21 sessions; breakevens are up 11.43bp; US two-year yields are up 48.30bp; VIX is 15.84; the FX vol ratio is 0.934. The rule permits normal exposure. I would describe that as carry permitted, rates repricing on watch, rather than infer that a quiet VIX proves the macro backdrop is safe. The two-year move is substantial, while the specified inflation-breakeven condition is not met.

For country selection, real yields, expected policy paths, external financing needs, terms of trade and credibility remain relevant. This dataset does not contain a consistent release-vintage panel for those variables. They are not secretly inferred from nominal carry, and no unmeasured country score is used to manufacture the picks. The operational selector below is market-based; those fundamental checks are the next layer of country underwriting, especially for EM.

2. Independent currency selection and sizing

The full universe is USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, NZD, CAD, CHF, JPY, NOK and SEK, plus optional MXN, BRL and HUF longs. EM is never used as funding. On each selection date:

  1. Derive observable carry from each currency's one-month forward versus USD. Compare yields on the same date; never apply today's yield to the past.
  2. DM longs must be above the DM median yield; funding currencies below it. Optional EM longs must clear the same yield threshold. Require pair carry of at least 0.5% annually.
  3. Report carry / vol as a ranking and compensation metric. For historical screening, use the maximum of 20-, 60- and 126-day realized pair vol. For current sizing, also impose a three-month implied-vol covariance constraint using all 12 currency/USD ATM quotes.
  4. Maximize annual forward carry less an explicit expected round-trip/roll-cost proxy, subject to covariance, factor, gross, currency and EM limits. Expected spot appreciation is set to zero in this carry-only objective; no regression intercept is treated as reliable alpha.
  5. Apply the macro multiplier and current currency-gap limits. Then express the resulting net currency exposures in a sparse set of crosses.

Carry / vol ranks attractiveness; it does not directly set dollar weights. If correlations were zero, unconstrained mean-variance sizing would be proportional to carry divided by variance, not carry divided by volatility. With correlated FX exposures, the marginal risk of an additional position depends on the whole portfolio. A high-scoring cross can be omitted because it duplicates an existing currency or factor exposure.

Covariance is built in currency space. For example, equal USD amounts of AUDCHF + GBPJPY can represent the same net currencies as AUDJPY + GBPCHF. Relabeling those crosses cannot reduce economic covariance or factor exposure. The code explicitly tests this invariance. Carry/vol is a tie-break for sparse cross routing after net exposures are chosen; it does not create extra diversification.

The normal limits are: $10m one-way capacity, $25k daily sigma; pair 25%, long currency 30%, funding currency 35%, EM 20% of the reference amount. Current gap limits tighten some of those caps. Thus the final weights are constrained outputs, not pure carry/vol weights and not maximum-Sharpe weights fitted to historical average returns.

3. Six-month factor model: useful, but not sufficient

For each currency cross, estimate simultaneously:

FX daily spot return = intercept + βe × SPX log return + βo × Brent log return + βr × change in US2y yield (bp) + residual

The core window is 126 aligned daily observations, refreshed with new closing data. Also inspect 63 and 252 observations and a downside-only equity regression. Simultaneous regression distinguishes a rates move from the part associated with oil/equities. It does not establish causality. Dollar exposures aggregate as the sum of each position's USD notional times its beta. A $1m SPX-equivalent exposure predicts approximately $10k P&L for a 1% SPX move, holding the other modeled factors fixed.

The sensitivity table is why I would use six months as the core, not the sole truth. Rates exposure is materially larger over the 3- and 12-month checks. With only three factors, most variance remains unexplained; relative monetary policy, currency-specific events, funding, positioning and nonlinear tails remain. Do not label the residual “pure carry alpha.” Generic Brent front-future history also contains roll effects, and same-date daily closes are not perfectly synchronized intraday marks.

For the $10m reference, the optimizer caps absolute SPX-equivalent exposure at $2m, oil at $0.5m, and US2y sensitivity at $1,500/bp. These correspond to $100k on a 5% equity move and $75k on a 15% oil move or 50bp rate move. Equity limits also apply to the 3- and 12-month estimates. Two joint scenarios—growth scare (SPX −5%, oil −15%, US2y −50bp), and inflation shock (−5%, +15%, +50bp)—have a $150k loss allowance.

Those factor limits are not binding for today's unconstrained solution. I have not tightened them afterward to force a claim that factor constraints transformed today's book. Historically they sometimes changed the portfolio. The current allocation changes mainly because of implied volatility and explicit currency-gap budgets. For the final DM book, modeled growth-scare loss is about $58.5k; that says nothing about a CHF policy jump outside the three-factor model.

4. Stops: four distinct controls, not a VaR stop

The original dollar ladder was a chosen inception-loss policy tested on historical paths. It was not derived from VaR, and its levels were not estimated to be optimal. The price review levels in the original report were volatility-based checkpoints, not validated independent stop signals.

The independent framework separates:

ControlTriggerAction
Carry / thesis invalidationPair carry falls below the entry hurdle; macro gate deteriorates; credible policy change invalidates the funding thesisRemove/re-underwrite the position; do not wait for the money stop
Ordinary portfolio riskUpdated daily sigma or factor exposure exceeds the defined budgetReduce/rebalance; this is exposure sizing, not a stop price
Currency jump riskSpecified currency shock would consume too much of the budgetConstrain the currency notional or use a separately priced bounded-loss structure before entry
Realized mandate lossInception P&L reaches −$150k / −$250k / −$350k / −$400kRatchet risk to 75% / 50% / 25% / zero; cancel unfilled adds

The current gap policy allows at most $250k, half the loss budget, in any single specified adverse currency move: 20% CHF or EM, 15% JPY, 10% other DM. These are policy stress sizes, not statistical quantiles or guaranteed worst cases. They imply CHF exposure at most $1.25m, JPY $1.667m, other DM $2.5m and each EM currency $1.25m. The actual model selected $1.25m BRL and $0.75m MXN when EM is permitted. Simultaneous currency shocks can still be worse.

This matters because the raw optimizer wants $3.5m CHF funding, a $700k loss under the specified 20% adverse CHF move. An ordinary covariance budget and low SPX beta do not solve that problem. Applying the gap limit reduces expected carry instead of disguising it as diversification. A January 2015 SNB spot replay of the resulting DM book is approximately −$223k, while its static acute-Covid replay is still −$522k before stop intervention.

The mixed factor model improves Sharpe from about 0.72 to 0.78 versus the same optimizer without factor constraints, but does not improve maximum drawdown. The macro gate lowers annual vol from 4.07% to 3.31% and the worst day from −2.19% to −1.36%; Sharpe falls to 0.74. This does not justify a claim of strong, persistent DM alpha.

At doubled costs, Sharpe falls to approximately 0.71 for mixed factor, 0.61 with the macro gate, and 0.30 for DM-only. The factor model uses realized covariance historically; current implied-vol and gap limits are subsequent current-risk overlays, separately stress-tested. The six-month window, broad budgets and initial macro rules were written before the new comparisons, but all research is retrospective. A DM-only diagnostic and the current-risk extensions were added after initial results; the specification records those additions. No winning parameter grid was selected.

7. Reproduction and limitations

The accompanying archive includes the full candidate screen, factor coefficients/uncertainty, current portfolios, daily macro states, annual/segment results, all campaign outcomes, data snapshots, request ledger, source and editable risk configuration. independent_config.json controls selection risk/currency/factor/gap budgets and the core factor window. config.json retains the inception-loss ladder and tranche schedule. Run python run_independent.py locally; it makes no Bloomberg calls. The narrative report is dated and is not automatically rewritten when parameters change.

The forward engine uses approximate log-linear front-curve marking, business-month-end maturity, USD policy-rate discounting and matched USD currency legs. It omits full historical broken-date curves, exact FX holiday/settlement calendars, collateral, historical executable bid/ask and NDF settlement details. Costs are 2bp DM / 5bp EM one-way, plus 1bp / 2bp monthly rolls. Sparse cross routing can add conservative implementation turnover even when some net exposures change little. Current synthetic IV uses historical correlations, not an observed implied correlation surface.

The revision passed 14 additional checks, including currency/covariance invariance, factor aggregation, no future-data leakage, current risk/factor/gap compliance and Bloomberg budget compliance, alongside the existing forward-engine checks. These validate calculations, not future returns. The archive is local; no market data or position information have been published externally.
