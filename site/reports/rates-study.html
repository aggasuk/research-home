Cache limitations and what is actually validated

The inventory inspected 1,511 persistent cache records. Forty-four records contain df=None (empty entries, not inaccessible valid histories). Among the inspected rate families, no usable TY/RX/JB daily PX_LAST history was found; available TY/RX records are mainly option volatility histories. The three government bond markets therefore remain yield-change proxies. Their carry and roll are not filled with unrelated swap curves.

Existing INR and KRW generic curves support the sensitivity above; a long BGN KRW history was not spliced into the generic family to manufacture earlier coverage. No prices, curves or short-end histories were fetched. Exact tradable total-return validation for the full basket is not possible with the inspected cache.

Checks passed: zero adjustment on a flat curve; upward-sloping par curve penalizes payers and benefits receivers; truncating future dates leaves earlier carry results unchanged; no transaction costs enter reported P&L; all used cache hashes unchanged. The original strategy checks cover delayed execution and future-price perturbations. Source hashes, coverage, rules, daily derived P&L and trade records are saved alongside this report.

Decision

Retain the frozen five-market pullback rule as the primary rates candidate, with established trend and breakout as recorded comparators. It is reasonable to observe its next signals in a research/paper record; it is not yet justified to badge them as proven profitable trades. Do not exclude Bund or restrict the rule to KRW after seeing these results. If the objective is a proven executable edge, the missing instrument economics and new independent observations remain real limitations; further parameter searches on the same cache will not resolve them.

No dashboard edits, deployment, recurring automation or live trading was performed. This completes the rates follow-up possible under cache-only/no-transaction-cost constraints.
